# How AI can fool you

AI errors are especially dangerous when they are plausible.

A fluent answer can feel like a conclusion before the research has actually happened.

> Asking an LLM to **“research this and tell me the truth”** without a research methodology is like asking a detective to solve a murder by interviewing one witness and then writing the closing argument.

**One witness is not an investigation. One citation is not a conclusion.**

Likewise, asking AI to **“find sources proving I’m right”** is not neutral research. It creates a confirmation-biased search objective: you are building a prosecution instead of investigating the question.

The working rule is simple:

> **Research the question—not your preferred answer.**

Common failure modes:

- fabricated or malformed citations;
- real citations attached to unsupported claims;
- summaries that omit caveats;
- source laundering through secondary articles;
- confirmation-biased searching;
- false agreement when many pages repeat one source;
- stale information presented as current;
- correlation described as causation;
- recommendation presented as fact;
- uncertainty disappearing after repeated summarization.

Treat AI output as a candidate synthesis. Verify the evidence chain.
