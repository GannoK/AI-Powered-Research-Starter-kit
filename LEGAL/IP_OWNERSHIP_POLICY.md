# Project IP Ownership and Chain-of-Title Policy

Status: active internal governance  
Owner of current project-specific copyrightable material: Kyle Gannon, unless and until a written assignment states otherwise.

## 1. Objective

Maintain a defensible chain of title for copyrightable software, research documentation, prompts, templates, examples, methodology expression, tooling, and related project assets while clearly separating owned material from licensed or third-party material.

## 2. Ownership rule

Material may be treated as project-owned only when at least one documented rights basis applies:

1. Kyle Gannon authored the copyrightable material;
2. the material was created by an employee within a legally qualifying scope of employment;
3. the material qualifies as a work made for hire under applicable law and the required written agreement exists;
4. the relevant rights were validly assigned in a signed writing; or
5. the rights were otherwise lawfully acquired and the acquisition is documented.

A Git commit, repository access, invoice, payment, conversation, pull request, attribution, or possession of a source file is not by itself sufficient evidence of copyright ownership.

## 3. Contributor intake

Before substantive contributor work is incorporated as project-owned IP:

- identify the contributor's legal name and contracting party;
- determine employee, contractor, vendor, collaborator, or licensor status;
- execute the appropriate written IP agreement;
- identify pre-existing/background IP;
- identify third-party dependencies;
- record any promised compensation or royalty rights in a separate signed agreement;
- retain executed copies outside the code repository in controlled business records.

## 4. Third-party material

Third-party material must be recorded in `../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` before production reliance or redistribution where practicable. Its license must permit the intended use.

Do not introduce copyleft, source-available, noncommercial, research-only, dataset, AI-model, or other restrictive material without explicit license review.

## 5. Background IP

Contributors must disclose pre-existing tools, libraries, examples, templates, data, text, designs, or other material they intend to incorporate. Background IP remains with its original owner unless expressly assigned. If the project needs continuing rights, obtain a written license broad enough for the intended use and distribution.

## 6. AI-assisted material

Follow `AI_ASSISTED_AUTHORSHIP_POLICY.md`. Do not assume all AI-generated output is copyrightable or exclusively ownable. Preserve records of meaningful human authorship, selection, arrangement, revision, and provenance where copyright status matters.

## 7. Revenue participation

Ownership and compensation are separate. No royalty, backend participation, revenue share, equity, or other contingent compensation is created by contribution, attribution, repository participation, or this policy. Such rights exist only when stated in a separate signed agreement.

## 8. IP transfers and entity changes

Any transfer of project IP from Kyle Gannon to Guild Technology Solutions, a dedicated IP holding company, buyer, licensee, or other entity must be documented by a written assignment or transaction document. Update `../COPYRIGHT.md`, relevant registrations, contracts, and this policy after the transfer becomes effective.

## 9. Records

Preserve:
- Git history and release boundaries;
- signed contributor/contractor/employment agreements;
- assignments and licenses;
- third-party license texts and versions;
- provenance records;
- commercial contracts;
- royalty statements and audit records where applicable;
- copyright/trademark registration records when obtained.

Do not silently rewrite historical authorship or licensing records.
